02 · Root Cause Analysis

Built for one vehicle.
Not for multi-agent convergence.

Three deep architectural blind spots in consumer mobile technology created this problem.

01

Single-Agent Isolation

Legacy maps (Google Maps, Apple Maps, Waze) were designed in the 2000s for a single driver moving from Point A to Point B. Their routing engines have zero concept of multiple distinct journeys converging toward a shared focal point.

[Car A] ➔ [Pin]
[A + B + C] ═► [Shared Pin]
02

The Spatial-to-Text Gap

Because maps couldn’t coordinate groups, people fell back to iMessage and WhatsApp. But text bubbles strip away 3D road geometry, turn complexity, and velocity—forcing drivers to guess ETAs while driving.

"5 mins away!" ➔ Stale Guess
Dynamic Island ➔ 60Hz Live Sync
03

The Surveillance Trap

Family trackers assumed people wanted 24/7 continuous background surveillance. But friends meeting for coffee don’t want to share their battery, driving speed, and lifetime whereabouts—they just want to coordinate arrival.

24/7 Stalking ➔ Intrusive
Ephemeral Session ➔ Privacy-First
